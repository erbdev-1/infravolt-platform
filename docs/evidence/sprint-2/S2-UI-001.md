It does not mark the mapped catalogue tasks complete where their wider acceptance depends on product/sales approval, verified company/contact disclosures, native-language review, future routes, production domains or later release-gate evidence.

No exact evidence identifier is prescribed for this cross-sprint visual slice. `S2-UI-001` is the first sequential Sprint 2 UI evidence record and does not replace the later G2 or G4 completion evidence.

## Implemented outcome

- Replaced the public placeholder with a responsive, server-rendered InfraVolt homepage for the trusted `uk` and `ua` market contexts.
- Added a typed content contract whose tuple structure keeps the two markets aligned across seven product systems, six sectors and four project-support steps.
- Added market-owned English and Ukrainian metadata, shell labels, page copy and section CTAs without URL locale prefixes or browser-controlled market selection.
- Added a reusable public shell with a utility/brand header, desktop anchor navigation, accessible mobile navigation, skip link, semantic landmarks and market-aware footer.
- Kept the page, shell, content and presentation components server-rendered. Only the mobile menu is a Client Component.
- Added a focused design foundation using Tailwind 4-compatible colour and breakpoint tokens, CSS custom properties, a local system-font stack, constrained containers, restrained radius/shadow rules, visible focus states and reduced-motion behavior.
- Added the hero, approved category summaries, sector summaries, project-support pathway, neutral Gersan systems section, final project CTA and current-year footer.
- Used CSS-led technical compositions because no approved image, video, document or logo asset was present. No external or restricted asset was introduced.
- Added safe market-aware title, description and canonical metadata from the existing validated market URL.

## Content and claim controls

- Product copy remains at category level and makes no specification, compliance, availability or completed-project claim.
- Gersan wording is descriptive and future-facing. It does not claim official authorisation, exclusivity, representation, territory rights, certification or global offices.
- Product details, documents, claims and media are explicitly held behind source, applicability and publication-rights verification.
- No Russia or Kaliningrad wording, fake metric, fake contact detail, fake company disclosure or fake working form is rendered.
- Every navigation and CTA link targets an existing homepage fragment; no future route was invented.

## Accessibility and component boundaries

- The document has one page-level `h1`, a logical heading hierarchy and semantic `header`, `nav`, `main`, `section` and `footer` landmarks.
- A keyboard-visible skip link targets the main content.
- The mobile menu uses a native button, accessible open/close names, `aria-expanded`, Escape handling, focus return, link-close behavior and reversible body-scroll locking.
- Controls use practical touch targets, focus indicators remain visible on light and dark surfaces, and information is not conveyed by colour alone.
- Decorative technical graphics are hidden from assistive technology while their meaning remains available in surrounding text.
- Reduced-motion preferences remove smooth scrolling, transitions and card movement.
- The only new `"use client"` boundary is `src/components/public/mobile-navigation.tsx`; it imports no server configuration or server-owned market resolver.

## Responsive visual review

Temporary full-page screenshots were captured for both market hosts at 375, 768 and 1440 CSS pixels, plus the open mobile menu in each language. They were inspected for hierarchy, wrapping, contrast, spacing, footer layout and empty areas, then removed.

The first mobile pass exposed a CSS cascade conflict that left the desktop header CTA visible and caused horizontal overflow. The selector was narrowed, all six required viewport checks were rerun, and final document `scrollWidth` did not exceed `clientWidth` in either market. Ukrainian expansion remained readable without clipped navigation or overlapping content.
